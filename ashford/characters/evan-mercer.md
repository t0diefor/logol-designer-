## The problem with his motivation — and the recommended fix

**The problem.** "Secretive because he wants to protect others" is a noble
reason, and noble reasons cost a character nothing. A hero who lies only out of
kindness is never wrong, never has to be forgiven, and never surprises anyone.
Worse, it produces the same scene on a loop: Evan lies to protect → Liam is
hurt by the lie → Evan apologises → repeat. Three issues of that and the reader
is ahead of the story.

**[PROPOSAL] — give the secrecy a second root that is not admirable.**
Keep the canon reason. Add a selfish one underneath it, and let Evan not quite
admit the second one to himself:

> Before the calling, Evan was planning to leave Ashford. Nothing dramatic —
> he just always assumed he was going. Being the Slayer is the first time he
> has mattered here, and telling anyone the truth would make it real,
> permanent, and about them.

What that buys, immediately:
- He can be **wrong**, which means he can change.
- The lie to Liam becomes two-sided — protecting Liam *and* protecting the one
  thing that is finally his.
- Season 1's reveal stops being "Evan apologises" and becomes "Liam realises
  Evan was going to leave."
- It gives the romance an actual stake: staying.

This is a proposal. It does not contradict anything canon — "brave, confident,
secretive to protect others" all remain true. It only adds a floor beneath
them. Mikey's call.

## Voice
Dry rather than quippy. Deflects with practicality — answers a feeling with a
task. Under pressure he gets *more* polite, which is how you know he is
frightened. He is funny the way exhausted people are funny, not the way
sitcom teenagers are.

**Dialogue discipline:** he should rarely say what he is feeling. Give the
feeling to the art.

## What the Cold does to him as a character

Worth stating, now that it is canon: the cost is **legible to other people
before it is legible to him.** Liam will feel cold hands. Kane will notice he
does not shiver. A dog will refuse him on Main Street. Evan is the last person
in Ashford to find out what is happening to his own body, which is a very
particular kind of horror and it is specific to this series.

It also means his heroism is subtractive. He does not level up — he spends.
