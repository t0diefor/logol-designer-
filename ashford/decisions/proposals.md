### P2 — The power is local to Ashford
Not one Slayer in all the world — one Slayer in *this town*, tied to this
coast. Solves a structural problem that will otherwise bite in Season 2
("why doesn't he just leave?"), makes the Watcher organization's interest in a
small town sinister rather than routine, and moves decisively away from the
global chosen-one frame. Does not change any confirmed canon.

### P3 — The organization is a mundane coastal bureaucracy
Not an ancient council. A maritime survey / salvage / lighthouse-service body
with filing cabinets and procedures. Julian carries a clipboard.
→ Full case in `characters/julian-blackwood.md`.

### P4 — Reshape the Season 3 test
**This is the most derivative element currently in the series, and it is worth
flagging bluntly:** "in Season 3 the secret organization subjects the Slayer to
a potentially fatal test, and the Watcher breaks with them to protect him" is
very close to a specific, well-known Season 3 arc in an existing franchise —
close in event, in season, and in motive.

Mikey's canon is that *Julian leaves the organization to protect Evan from a
potentially fatal test*. That event can stay exactly as it is. What should
change is **what the test is**:

> It is not a rite, an ordeal, or a trial of worthiness. It is a **succession
> procedure**. The organization does not want to test whether Evan is strong
> enough — it wants a Slayer it can file, and Evan has become unmanageable. The
> "test" is the mechanism by which the post becomes vacant.
>
> Julian leaves because he works out what the paperwork is actually for.

This keeps every confirmed fact, makes the horror administrative rather than
mystical, pays off P3, and is not borrowed from anywhere.

## On originality generally

Three honest notes:

1. The elements most likely to read as borrowed are, in order: **the Season 3
   test (P4)**, **the Watcher/council structure (P3)**, and **the global
   chosen-one framing (P2)**. All three can be changed without touching a
   single confirmed fact, which is unusually lucky.
2. The terms "Slayer" and "Watcher" are confirmed canon and have not been
   changed or proposed for change. They are also the most recognisable words in
   the project. That is Mikey's decision to make, not Claude's, and it is worth
   making consciously rather than by default.
3. **Claude cannot assess legal risk and makes no claim about it.** These notes
   are craft observations about how the work will read, nothing more. A real
   answer on clearance requires a lawyer.
