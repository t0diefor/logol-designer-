## The previous Slayer [PROPOSAL]

She needs a name, a job and about one page of life. Proposed: **Roz**, 24,
night shift at the Ashford fish plant. Surname open — Mikey should pick, since
this is the first new name in the series.

Why a 24-year-old plant worker rather than someone glamorous: it establishes
immediately that being the Slayer in Ashford is not a destiny, it is a night
job nobody thanks you for. That single choice does more anti-cliché work than
any amount of mythology.

**Craft decision, stated plainly:** she dies at the end of **page 1**, not page
3. In a 16-page issue you cannot spend three pages before meeting the lead. One
page of competence — the coveralls, the rebar, the line "Third one this
month" — buys enough for her death to land. If Mikey wants her death to carry
more weight, the right fix is to *see her again in flashback later*, not to
spend pages 2–3 on her now.

## Deliberate restraint in this issue

- **Julian explains nothing.** Every instinct will push toward a page of
  mythology in the 11–13 slot. Resisting it is what makes #1 a mystery instead
  of a briefing, and it gives Issue #2 something to be about.
- **The word "vampire" is never spoken.** Not by Evan, not by Julian, not in a
  caption.
- **Seraphine does not appear.** A 200-year-old who turns up in issue one to
  menace a teenager is not frightening. She should be a consequence, later.
- **The hunting outfit does not appear.** Evan has no idea he is a hunter. The
  outfit, the stakes, the vials and the pendant are an Issue #3-or-later image
  and should be saved — the first time a reader sees that silhouette, it should
  mean he has accepted something.
- **Liam does not find out.** That is a later-in-Season-1 canon event.

## Quality check against Mikey's criteria
- *Contradicts canon?* No. Evan is human, ignorant at the start, knows by the end. Liam is a civilian. Julian is assigned and cold. Nobody's appearance is changed.
- *Does each scene change the situation?* Yes — see the right-hand column.
- *Motivated choices?* The weak link is page 14: **why does Evan go to the waterfront at night?** It must not be curiosity. See Q5 in `decisions/open-questions.md`; the current best answer is that Liam asks him to, which makes the lie start on the same night as the power.
- *Dialogue distinct and concise?* Target: under 25 words per page for pages 1–3 and 14–16.
- *Drawable?* Yes. Four locations total across sixteen pages: breakwater, Evan's room, school, waterfront. That is a cheap issue to draw, on purpose.
- *Pacing of romance and revelation?* No romance beat lands in #1 beyond proximity and one unfinished sentence. The supernatural lands on the last two pages. Both correct for a first issue.
- *Realistic workload?* 16 pages, 4 locations, 2 named designs needed, 1 creature design that is mostly withheld.
