# ASHFORD — Issue #1, Page 1

### ⚠ STATUS: [DRAFT PROPOSAL] — for Mikey's approval
New in this page: the character **Roz** and the line "Third one this month."
Both are proposals, not canon.

---

## Dramatic purpose

Teach the reader three things without a word of explanation:

1. **Ashford is a working town.** Night shift, rain, sodium light, a woman in
   plant coveralls. Not a haunted village.
2. **The thing in the fog is not human** — and the reader learns it from one
   detail, not from a monster reveal: everyone's breath fogs in this cold.
   Its breath does not.
3. **The Slayer can lose.** She is competent, she is calm, she has done this
   before, and it is not enough.

The page also plants the mechanic the whole series will run on, before the
series has explained that it has one.

## Panel count and layout

**5 panels.** Suggested grid:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│  1   wide establishing          │   ~1/4 page
├───────────────┬─────────────────┤
│  2            │  3              │   ~1/4 page
├───────────────┴─────────────────┤
│  4   narrow horizontal          │   ~1/5 page, tight
├─────────────────────────────────┤
│  5   wide, low angle            │   remainder — biggest panel
└─────────────────────────────────┘
```

Panel 4 is deliberately short and wide — a held breath before the last panel.
Panel 5 should be the largest on the page.

---

## PANEL 1

**ART:** Wide establishing shot. Night. The Ashford breakwater — a concrete
arm reaching out into the inlet, slick with rain. Sodium lamps at intervals,
each one a cone of orange in heavy fog; the light does not reach far. Behind
and to the left, the bulk of the fish plant: corrugated siding, a few lit
windows, a stack. Rain falling steadily, visible against the lamps. A single
small figure is walking out along the breakwater, away from us, hands in
pockets. She is tiny in frame. Nothing is threatening yet. It is just a cold
night at work.

> **CAPTION (top left):** Ashford, British Columbia.
> **CAPTION (below it):** Eleven minutes after shift change.

---

## PANEL 2

**ART:** Much closer. **ROZ**, 24, in fish-plant coveralls with the sleeves
pushed up, hair pulled back and soaked flat, a two-foot length of rebar held
loose at her side the way you hold a tool you have carried a hundred times —
not a weapon, not raised. She has stopped walking. Head slightly turned,
chin up, eyes off-panel left: *listening*, not looking. Her breath plumes
visibly in the cold. Relaxed shoulders. She is not afraid.

> **ROZ:** Third one this month.

---

## PANEL 3

**ART:** Action. One beat only. Roz pivots on her back foot and drives the
rebar forward and slightly up into something lunging at her out of the fog
from panel left. We see **almost nothing** of the attacker — a shoulder, an
arm, a pale hand, the edge of a coat, all of it cut by fog and the frame. Her
face is set and businesslike, not snarling. The impact is clean and
unglamorous. Rain streaks across the whole panel.

> **SFX:** *K-SHUNK*

---

## PANEL 4

**ART:** Tight horizontal two-shot, side on, faces close. The attacker has
hold of Roz's forearm. We are close enough to see both of their mouths and the
air between them. **Roz's breath is fogging. The attacker's is not.** Nothing
else in the panel is strange: the hand on her arm is just a hand, the face is
just a face in shadow — if anything it should look disappointingly ordinary.
Her expression changes here for the first time on the page: not terror, but
the small flat recognition of someone realising they have made an error.

> *(No dialogue. No SFX. Silence is the point — this panel should be the
> quietest on the page.)*

---

## PANEL 5

**ART:** Wide, low angle, camera almost at deck level. Roz is down on the wet
concrete in the foreground, on her side, face toward us, eyes open. The rebar
has rolled a short way off and come to rest against the lip of the breakwater.
Rain pocking the puddles around her. The attacker is **gone** — the fog behind
her is empty, which should feel worse than seeing it leave.

The one piece of craft the panel lives on: a faint plume of breath rising from
her mouth and thinning as it goes up. We are watching the last one.

> **SFX (small, scattered through the rain):** *tk tk tk tk*

---

## Lettering and reading order

- Both captions sit top-left of panel 1, stacked, so the eye starts there and
  travels out along the breakwater with the figure.
- Panel 2 is the only normal dialogue balloon on the page. Keep it small and
  place it right of her head so the eye continues left-to-right into panel 3.
- The *K-SHUNK* in panel 3 should break the panel border on the left — the
  direction the attack comes from.
- **Panel 4 gets no lettering at all.** If a letterer asks to put something
  there, the answer is no.
- Panel 5's rain SFX should be small, grey, and scattered — not a display SFX.
  It is texture, not an announcement.

**Total word count: 11.** That is intentional.

## Page-turn hook

The page ends on a breath thinning into nothing. The reader turns the page
expecting to find out what killed her.

**Page 2 does not answer that.** It follows the breath. (See
`story/issue-01-outline.md`, pages 2–3.)
