## 2. [PROPOSAL] The cost: *the Cold*

**Awaiting Mikey's yes / no / modify. Do not script around it until confirmed.**

The power runs on body heat. When Evan draws on it, he stops producing warmth.

What that looks like on the page, concretely:

- **His breath stops fogging.** In a town that is cold and wet nine months of
  the year, everyone else's breath plumes and his does not. This is drawable in
  a single panel with no dialogue, and it is the same detail that marks the
  thing that kills the previous Slayer on page 1 — so the reader learns the
  rule from the monster before Evan ever uses it.
- **His hands go cold.** Someone holding his hand notices. Liam notices.
- **Animals avoid him** after heavy use. Dogs on Main Street, gulls on the
  waterfront.
- **Recovery is slow and physical.** He eats constantly, shivers, sleeps badly.
  He is not injured; he is running down.

Why this proposal rather than another:

1. **It obstructs the romance without a contrivance.** The slow burn needs a
   real obstacle. "He is cold to the touch and cannot explain why" is an
   obstacle that lives in his body, cannot be talked around, and gets worse the
   more heroic he is.
2. **It makes him resemble the enemy.** The thing the reader is taught to fear
   on page 1 is identified by not breathing fog. By page 40 Evan has the same
   tell. That is a horror engine and a character engine at once, and it gives
   Inspector Kane something real to be suspicious about.
3. **It is cheap to draw and impossible to forget.** One visual, repeatable,
   unmistakable, and native to a cold wet coastal town. It is not borrowed.

**Compatible add-on, also a proposal:** the power is weakest at slack water —
the twenty minutes at the turn of the tide when nothing is moving. Tide tables
are public, so both sides can plan around them, which makes the schedule a
story device rather than an excuse.

### Deliberately not proposed

- No "the power makes him violent / someone else inside him." Common, and it
  removes responsibility from his choices.
- No prophecy. Prophecy tells the reader the ending.
- No power-up ladder. The powers should stay roughly where they are; what
  changes is what Evan is willing to spend.
